This book is a **great read**, and I gotta give props to the author for the way he wrote it because we read it from the perspective of a student talking to a Professor, and we have access to the *inner thoughts of the student* which might in many times parallel what we are thinking ourselves, hence we have this experience of being *accompanied and answered* all throughout the book.

**Adler's** ideas are pretty interesting if we take into consideration today's world. We revolve so much around **trauma** and how that shapes people and he completely denies that idea. Its the whole concept of your dad being an alcoholic and you either follow his footsteps or learn from them and try to do better.

Its take on all the problems we have being **interpersonal problems** its also pretty interesting, and such a weird idea because I might have some problems which I consider to only be my own such as insecurities and stuff. But those insecurities only exist in the light of *comparison with others* hence all our problems being interpersonal ones.

Also, **"Separation of Tasks"** there's stuff that my responsibility and other things are simply others responsibility. We humans tend to either make everything about ourselves or everything about others. Like a father who's children does not want to study, it is our *"task"* to advise him to study for it is an important manner which can drive the way we live our life. But we must not be so presumptuous or prideful that we believe it is our duty to *"make"* that child study. Because that is his/her task, we may only do so far as our tasks at the risk of being unsatisfied with everything around us. And for this we must keep a balance of *"doing as much to help others"* and *"not trying to do things for others that are their tasks"*. It's the old idea of *"give a fish to a man and he'll have dinner for day, teach him how to fish and he'll have dinner for life"*.
